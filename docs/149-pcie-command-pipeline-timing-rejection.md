## Measured result — 6 October 2026

The V25 packet-integrity candidate moves the parser-to-ring command behind
registers. Its corrected functional campaign passes **43 tests**, including
fault, reset, burst and command-order checks. The unchanged 4 ns preplacement
screen still fails: slow-corner setup slack is **−3.421986 ns**, 230.304 ps
worse than V23. **V25 is rejected and is not adopted into the chip.** V11
remains the stable baseline; that designation does not mean timing is closed.

The [delivery inventory](../hw/soc/pcie-evidence/20261006-integrity-command-pipeline/delivery-inventory.json)
binds the ten product source/test files, complete captures, independent
reviews and immutable public download receipts. The failed initial campaign
remains separate from the corrected campaign. This is a reproducible record
of the change and its rejection, not a claim of PHY or chip completion.

## Registered command and fault behavior

The [generator](../scripts/generate_pcie_integrity_command_v25.py) registers
the complete ring-write command generated by the V23 parser. The previous
command is applied even when the parser is idle or finishing a packet. Ring
payload, descriptor and verdict operations retain their order, and the
visible commit waits for the corresponding write. Nominal payload output
has one additional cycle of latency in the two explicitly checked cases.
General cycle equivalence is not claimed.

The first campaign produced **40 passes and two failures**. One exposed a
reset-X delta-cycle problem: writes could occur while a reset control was
unknown. Explicit reset and enable guards were added to command application,
command consumption and cache updates. A component mutation removes that
guard and reproduces the unwanted write.

The other failure came from a fault-test prelude that filled 61 of 64 ring
slots before reaching the intended fault. The same prelude overflowed V23.
The corrected test uses the established passing ring-64 cadence, then applies
the fault. Separate ring-16, MAX-18 tests retain line-rate traffic, deliberate
backpressure overflow and restart coverage. The maximum MAX-4118 case is
explicitly excluded and remains open; passing the smaller override does not
close it. Both original failed captures remain available.

The corrected campaign includes nineteen direct and nineteen instrumented
transaction profiles, 4,102 component relations and twelve component fault
mutations. Twelve public-interface, eight architecture and two block-level
mutations exercise the intended failures. Unknown-control holds, fault-epoch
restart, stale-tag protection and descriptor wrap are checked within the
recorded scenarios. These results do not establish arbitrary four-state
behavior or complete protocol conformance.

## Actual mapping and timing rejection

All seven mapping/import/STA stages complete with successful tool exits.
The imported graph contains **95,243 cells and 9,130 flip-flops**, including
200 descriptor payload flip-flops. An independent reader checks the graph,
source correspondence and complete saved timing reports. Successful tool
execution is distinct from a passing timing result.

| Corner | Setup slack | Hold slack |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Slow | −3.421986 ns | +0.401006 ns |
| Typical | −0.704524 ns | +0.260212 ns |
| Fast | +0.887832 ns | +0.176000 ns |

The critical slow-corner path runs from `remaining[4]` to `verdict[112][0]`.
Its reported delay includes 0.234542 ns at the launch flip-flop, 1.972544 ns
across thirteen buffers, 4.434161 ns across thirty-one Boolean cells and
0.581255 ns across three muxes. The registered payload command has not
removed the current-token fault/control path into verdict writing. This
measured netlist path guides the next repair; a source-level attribution
alone would not establish a timing improvement.

The comparison uses the same 4 ns clock and unchanged I/O constraints.
There is no clock relaxation, false-path waiver or hold exemption. Slow
setup is 230.304 ps worse than V23, and typical setup also remains negative.
The positive preplacement hold figures do not close routed hold timing.
Mapped functional replay, clock-tree construction, route extraction and
main-chip integration have not been performed for this rejected candidate.

## Complete evidence and remaining work

Four archives preserve 1,562 members: the original failed campaign, the
corrected functional campaign, the native timing run and the review
supplement. Those four archives and their package manifest are published as
five immutable assets, each with complete authenticated and anonymous
readback. Current functional and native archived source paths still match
their origins. Six changed paths in the historical failed archive are
explicitly listed; the archived historical bytes themselves remain intact.

The independent checks preserve the distinction between the two campaigns:
85 historical test executions are **83 passes and two failures**, while the
corrected campaign alone is 43 passes. They are not 85 distinct passing tests.
The [loaded-divider numerical result](147-pcie-loaded-divider-numerical-agreement.md)
and [independent PLL publication repair](148-pcie-local-publisher-log-race.md)
have separate evidence and acceptance limits. Complete PLL/CDR, qualified
analog/RC models, full PCIe PHY integration and final chip setup/hold/LVS
acceptance remain open.
